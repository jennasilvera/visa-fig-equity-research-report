# Account-to-Account Payment Risk

## Purpose

Account-to-account payments are one of the most important structural risks to card networks. A2A payments allow money to move directly from a consumer or business bank account to another bank account, potentially bypassing card networks.

The key question is:

> Which Visa use cases are vulnerable to A2A substitution, and which remain protected by card-network functionality?

## Why A2A Matters

A2A payments can be attractive because they may offer lower merchant cost, direct bank-account funding, faster settlement, reduced dependence on card networks, government or bank support, and strong domestic use-case fit.

## Why A2A May Be Overstated

A2A does not automatically replicate the full card value proposition. Card networks offer credit access, rewards, chargebacks, dispute rights, fraud protection, global acceptance, consumer habit, authorization reliability, and card-not-present acceptance.

## A2A Risk by Use Case

| Use Case | A2A Threat Level | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bill pay | High | Low-risk recurring payment suited to bank transfer |
| Account funding | High | Natural bank-transfer use case |
| P2P | High | Users may prioritize speed and low cost |
| Payroll / disbursements | Medium/High | Faster bank payment attractive |
| Merchant debit transactions | Medium/High | Merchants may steer to lower-cost bank payments |
| Ecommerce credit purchases | Low/Medium | Credit, rewards, fraud protection, and disputes matter |
| Travel | Low/Medium | Cross-border and disputes matter |
| B2B payments | Medium/High | Bank rails are already common, but digitization opportunity exists |

## Model Treatment

A2A risk should affect payments volume, net revenue yield, client incentives, operating margin, terminal multiple, and VAS offset assumptions in bear and severe downside cases.
