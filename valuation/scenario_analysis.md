# Scenario Analysis

## Purpose

Scenario analysis is essential for Visa because the company has high quality fundamentals but meaningful regulatory, competitive, and valuation risks.

A professional investor does not only ask what the base case is. They ask:

> What has to be true for the stock to work, what could break the thesis, and what is the downside if the market changes its view?

## Scenario Framework

The V2 model should include four scenarios:

1. Bull Case
2. Base Case
3. Bear Case
4. Severe Downside Case

## Bull Case

The bull case assumes Visa is increasingly valued as a global trust, risk, tokenization, and money movement platform.

Key assumptions:

- VAS growth remains elevated
- Visa Direct gains traction
- Cross-border volume remains resilient
- Client incentives remain controlled
- Operating margins remain strong
- Buybacks support EPS growth
- Terminal multiple remains premium or expands

## Base Case

The base case assumes Visa remains a durable global payments compounder.

Key assumptions:

- Net revenue grows at a healthy rate
- Operating margin remains stable
- VAS grows faster than core revenue
- Cross-border growth normalizes but remains positive
- Buybacks continue
- Regulatory risk remains manageable
- Valuation remains premium but disciplined

## Bear Case

The bear case assumes pressure from regulation, incentives, and alternative rails.

Key assumptions:

- Client incentives rise faster than gross revenue
- Net revenue yield declines
- Cross-border growth slows
- A2A and RTP pressure selected debit-like use cases
- VAS growth slows
- Operating margin compresses
- Terminal P/E multiple declines

## Severe Downside Case

The severe downside case assumes a combination of macro, regulatory, and competitive pressure.

Key assumptions:

- Regulatory shock affects network economics
- Cross-border volume weakens materially
- Client incentives increase sharply
- Payment volume growth slows
- VAS fails to offset core pressure
- Multiple compresses materially
- Buybacks are less effective at protecting EPS

## Model Drivers

| Driver | Bull | Base | Bear | Severe Downside |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue Growth | Higher | Normalized | Lower | Materially lower |
| Operating Margin | Stable / higher | Stable | Lower | Materially lower |
| Client Incentives | Controlled | Manageable | Higher | Significantly higher |
| VAS Growth | Strong | Healthy | Slower | Weak |
| Cross-Border Growth | Strong | Normalized | Slower | Shock |
| Terminal Multiple | Premium | Premium but disciplined | Lower | Compressed |

## Investment Use

Scenario analysis should directly inform:

- Target price
- Downside case
- Upside case
- Risk/reward
- Recommendation
- What would change the thesis

## Interview Talking Point

> My base case is positive, but I explicitly model regulatory and take-rate downside. The bear case is not that Visa suddenly becomes a bad business. The more realistic risk is that investors lower the terminal multiple if they lose confidence in take-rate durability.
